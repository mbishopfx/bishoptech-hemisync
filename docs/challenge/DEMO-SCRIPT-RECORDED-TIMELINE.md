# Cognistration — voiceover for the recorded timeline

This is the primary voiceover for the screen recording that was actually
captured. It is written to sit under the UI instead of narrating every click.
Let the visible prompt, controls, guide pages, signup form, purchase card, and
App Store button carry their own proof.

The raw sequence runs about 2:38 before the 28-second MCP catalog outro, which
would make a 3:06 final cut. Use the trim plan at the end to submit a version
under three minutes without removing the ending.

## 0:00–0:05 — Cognistration home

> Cognistration starts with a feeling, not a playlist—a cue I can shape, save,
> and return to.

## 0:05–0:34 — ChatGPT app and bedtime request

> I’m asking the agent for a bedtime tone and the interactive generator. Many
> meditation apps start with a finished track, melody, or preset frequency.
> Cognistration starts with the person: every brain, body, and context is
> different. A binaural cue uses nearby tones—one in each ear—and I can stay in
> control, tuning the cue to what feels right instead of being pushed toward one
> frequency. The agent understands my intention and brings the machine into the
> conversation. Playback still waits for my say-so.

## 0:34–0:39 — Transition into the machine

> Now the machine and the explanation are side by side.

## 0:39–1:13 — Generator and first guide

> The generator is on the right and the guide is on the left. I can start the
> preview, move through the guide, and keep the session in place. The machine
> exposes direction, carrier, beat, and volume. FFR, or frequency-following
> response, is a measurable neural response to repeating sound features, studied
> with EEG or MEG. We are not measuring my brain live. The controls give me a
> direct way to explore an entrainment goal: when outside noise recedes and I
> feel more in tune with body and mind. I can tune, save, and return to what
> fits—not a forced state.

## 1:14–1:35 — Second science guide

> If I want more context, I can ask for a science guide. One 2021 MEG and EEG
> study of 20 adults found the auditory response converging toward the stimulus
> frequency in roughly 100 milliseconds. That is entrainment as a measurable
> timing effect—not a whole-brain theta or alpha switch. The wider
> binaural-beat evidence is mixed, so the guide keeps the claim narrow.

## 1:37–1:55 — Account signup

> I can also ask to create an account. The signup form appears inside the app,
> but I enter the credentials myself. They are not passed through MCP, and
> nothing is submitted until I choose to submit it.

## 2:00–2:38 — Tone purchase and iPhone offer

> Then I ask whether I can buy a tone. The agent explains the option in plain
> language first, and when I ask for the user experience, it opens the mini
> checkout app. I can copy the prompt to continue, review the delivery path,
> and see the real iPhone App Store option too.

## 2:38–3:06 — MCP catalog outro

> To wrap it up, Cognistration turns an intention into a controllable listening
> session through MCP. Every listener can be different. That is why this is a
> free-will generator: I discover a cue, tune it to what feels right, save the
> recipe, and return to it instead of accepting one fixed track or frequency.
> The agent can recommend and compare tones, plan a session, adjust the machine,
> open the FFR guide, and surface signup, downloads, feedback, and checkout. I
> control playback and sensitive actions, while the science stays
> educational—not a promise.

## Science fact-check — not spoken

The added FFR timing detail comes from Coffey et al.’s 2021 MEG/EEG study:
20 adults were included in the repeated-syllable experiment, the response
converged toward the stimulus over about 100 milliseconds, and a second
experiment tested tone transitions in 30 people. The FFR definition and the
periodic-input measurement language are consistent with Zhu et al.’s auditory
neuroscience study. The evidence boundary reflects López-Caballero and
Escera’s controlled study, which found no significant increase in the targeted
EEG bands or arousal measures for the tested binaural beats.

The script uses “many meditation apps” and “fixed track or frequency” as a
product comparison, not a universal claim about every app. “Free-will design”
describes user agency: tuning a cue does not guarantee entrainment or a
particular mental state.

- [Coffey et al., “Oscillatory Entrainment of the Frequency-following Response in Auditory Cortical and Subcortical Structures”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8176755/)
- [Zhu et al., “A comparison of spectral magnitude and phase-locking value analyses of the frequency-following response to complex tones”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3724813/)
- [López-Caballero and Escera, “Binaural Beat: A Failure to Enhance EEG Power and Emotional Arousal”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5694826/)
